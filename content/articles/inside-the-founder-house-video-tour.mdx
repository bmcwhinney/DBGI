A converted guesthouse two streets back from the Roseau waterfront has become an unlikely proving ground for the island's startup scene. Twelve founders live and work there under a residency programme run by the accelerator, sharing a single unreliable internet connection and, more usefully, a running conversation about what actually works when you are building for a market this size.

The video above walks through a typical week: pitch rehearsals on the veranda, a supply chain call with a distributor in Barbados, and the kind of late-night debugging session that looks the same in Roseau as it does anywhere else.

What comes through most clearly is how much the founders lean on each other. With no dense local network of experienced operators to call on, the house has become the substitute, a place where a fintech founder can get product feedback from someone building in agritech simply because they are eating breakfast at the same table.

Three ventures from this year's cohort have since closed seed rounds, a detail covered in more depth elsewhere in this section.
